**Why Google sees a Soft 404 on pages that live-test clean** (*per the live site*): the server sends full prerendered HTML (3,344 words, correct `<title>`, `<h1>`, canonical, 6 JSON-LD blocks; identical shape to the indexed caustic hub), and headless Chromium as Googlebot renders both hubs with zero console errors and zero failed requests. But `src/main.tsx` mounts with `createRoot().render()`, not hydration, so the first thing React does is **replace the prerendered content with the `<Loading />` spinner** (an image and three dots, no text) until the lazy route chunk resolves. Measured spinner window on a healthy fetch: ~250 ms, even at 6× CPU throttling on slow 3G. So a Soft 404 needs the route chunk to fail or time out inside Google's renderer — which is precisely the intermittent chunk-fetch failure diagnosed on 2026-08-31 (`lazyRetry.ts` header), and `lazyRetry`'s recovery is a `location.reload()`, which a crawler renderer will not follow. Every page crawled between a deploy and Google's JS cache refresh is exposed to it; the three hubs and the Oman page were all touched on Oct 5–12 and crawled Oct 8–13. Request indexing clears the symptom; keeping the prerendered content in the DOM until the chunk is ready would remove the cause.
